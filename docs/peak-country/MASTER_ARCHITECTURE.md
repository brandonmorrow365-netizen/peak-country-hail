# Peak Country Auto Hail Repair & Paintless Dent Repair

## Master Website Architecture — Baseline Specification

**Status:** Working master specification
**Purpose:** Define what the website must accomplish before detailed workflow design, final visual refinement, and repository-wide optimization.

---

# 1. OVERALL WEBSITE PHILOSOPHY

The Peak Country website should feel like a **premium professional service business operated by an experienced PDR technician**, not an automated estimating website or generic AI-generated service template.

The website's purpose is to:

- build trust
- demonstrate real experience
- show real repair results
- educate customers without overwhelming them
- collect useful lead information with minimal friction
- help customers understand their options
- route customers toward the correct service
- distinguish normal repair discussions from professional estimating/documentation services
- support insurance-related repairs without becoming an insurance-claim funnel
- generate qualified repair opportunities
- make Peak Country easy for search engines and AI systems to understand
- preserve human interaction before customers spend money

The website should automate administrative tasks while **not automating away the personal expertise that differentiates Peak Country**.

The central philosophy is:

**Conversation before transaction.**

---

# 2. PRIMARY CUSTOMER JOURNEY

The overall customer journey should be:

**Visitor lands on site**

→ understands what Peak Country does

→ sees real repair work and proof of experience

→ clicks **Get Started**

→ identifies what type of damage they have

→ provides basic customer and vehicle information

→ answers insurance-related questions when applicable

→ submits request

→ receives a personalized acknowledgment

→ may upload photos, request an inspection, review estimating options, or ask to speak first

→ Peak Country reviews the request

→ Peak Country speaks with the customer

→ appropriate repair/estimate path is determined

→ paid estimating service is offered only when appropriate

→ customer approves any estimating fee

→ payment is collected immediately before professional estimating/documentation begins

→ repair scope is developed

→ customer authorizes repair

→ repair is completed

→ repair payment is handled according to the repair agreement.

The website should progressively move customers through the process rather than demanding everything at once.

## **Get Started**

- Checkout

# 6. INITIAL CUSTOMER INFORMATION

The first intake should collect only information necessary to create a useful lead.

Working information requirements:

- Name
- Phone and/or email
- Vehicle
- Type of damage

At least one reliable contact method must be provided.

Phone should not necessarily be mandatory if the customer provides email.

A later workflow decision can determine whether email is required or whether either contact method is sufficient.

## Vehicle information

The site should collect basic vehicle information without making the first form cumbersome.

Exact field structure will be determined during workflow design.

## Photos

Photos are:

**Optional during initial intake.**

The customer should not be prevented from submitting a request simply because they do not currently have photographs.

Suggested language:

**Photos are optional, but they may help us give you better guidance.**

Customers should also have another opportunity to upload photographs after submitting the initial request.

---

# 7. PRIVACY / TRUST MESSAGE

The initial form should include simple customer-friendly reassurance.

Concept:

**Your information is only used to respond to your request. No spam. No marketing lists. We do not sell your information.**

Final wording should remain concise and accurate.

The purpose is to reduce hesitation about submitting contact information.

---

# 8. POST-SUBMISSION EXPERIENCE

Submitting the first form should not immediately lead to checkout.

The customer should receive a personalized response based on the service they selected.

Example concept:

**Thanks for contacting Peak Country about your hail damage. We appreciate the opportunity to help. We'll review what you've shared and help determine the best next step.**

Potential next actions may include:

- Upload Photos
- Talk to Us First
- Request an Inspection
- Review Estimate Options

The exact number, ordering, and behavior of these choices will be defined in the separate workflow specification.

## Important distinction

Use:

### **Request an Inspection**

rather than immediately allowing unrestricted appointment booking.

Peak Country should normally speak with the customer first so the correct service and amount of time can be determined before confirming an appointment.

# 9. INSURANCE QUESTION

When insurance may affect the repair process, particularly with hail damage, ask:

## Are you planning to use insurance?

Choices:

- Yes
- No
- Not Sure

Supporting explanation should communicate:

**Insurance repairs require a more formal estimating and documentation process and may take longer to prepare. Using insurance does not change Peak Country's repair quality or service standards.**

Insurance should affect:

- documentation
- estimating process
- timeframe
- administrative requirements

It should **not imply different repair quality**.

# 10. EXISTING INSURANCE ESTIMATE ROUTING

When a hail customer selects that they plan to use insurance, the website should eventually determine:

## Do you already have an insurance company or body shop estimate?

Choices:

### Yes — I already have an estimate

Potential path toward:

**Hail Insurance / Supplement Documentation**

### No — I don't have an estimate yet

Potential path toward:

**Comprehensive Hail Damage Estimate**

### I'm Not Sure

# 11. CORE ESTIMATING PHILOSOPHY

Peak Country should not communicate that it charges customers simply to look at ordinary dents.

There is an important distinction between:

### Normal repair evaluation

and

### Professional written estimating/documentation services.

Routine retail PDR normally begins with a:

## Free Initial Assessment

Paid services apply when substantially greater professional inspection, documentation, photography, insurance analysis, or written repair preparation is required.

# 12. FREE INITIAL ASSESSMENT

## Price

**$0**

This is the recommended starting point for most customers.

Appropriate for:

- ordinary dents
- door dings
- creases
- retail PDR
- customers exploring hail repair
- customers unsure whether repair is appropriate
- customers looking for general pricing direction
- customers who want to talk to an experienced PDR technician first

It may include:

- conversation
- review of photographs
- brief visual inspection
- discussion of repair options
- general price range where practical
- recommendation regarding next steps

It is not:

- a formal written estimate
- an insurance estimate
- an itemized repair document
- a detailed hail inspection
- a guaranteed final repair price

Suggested positioning:

## Free Initial Assessment

**Recommended Starting Point**

Start with a conversation. We'll review the damage, discuss your options, and provide general pricing direction where possible.

**No obligation to proceed.**

- photographic documentation

- review of body-shop documentation

- identification of missing operations

# 15. COMPREHENSIVE HAIL DAMAGE ESTIMATE

A comprehensive hail estimate requires substantially more professional time than a routine PDR evaluation.

## Working price

### **$249–$299**

## Approximate professional time

### **120 minutes**

This service may include:

- dent counts
- dent sizing

- repair operations
- applicable R&I considerations
- photographs
- detailed written documentation
- formal repair planning
- insurance-level documentation where appropriate

# 17. ESTIMATING PAYMENT TIMING

This rule is critical.

## Submitting a request does not create a charge.

The required sequence is:

**Customer submits request**

→ Peak Country reviews request

→ Peak Country speaks with customer

→ appropriate service is identified

→ exact fee is explained

→ customer agrees

→ payment is collected

→ professional estimating/documentation begins.

Payment for paid estimating services is due **at the beginning of the estimating service**, after the customer has spoken with Peak Country.

It is not due when the customer:

- clicks Get Started
- submits the website form
- uploads photographs
- asks about insurance
- requests an inspection
- asks for information

The website should reinforce:

**Talk first. Determine what you actually need. Pay only when a professional paid estimating service is agreed upon.**

---

# 18. REPAIR PAYMENT

Repair payment is separate from estimating-service payment.

The normal repair path remains:

**Repair scope established**

→ customer authorizes repair

→ repair is completed

→ payment according to the repair agreement.

Purchasing an estimating service does not require the customer to prepay the repair.

---

# 19. ESTIMATE-FEE CREDIT

When Peak Country performs the repair substantially as estimated, the applicable paid estimating/documentation fee should be credited toward that repair.

## Repair substantially as written

Applicable estimating fee is credited.

## Repair materially changed

If the customer:

- removes significant portions of the repair
- selects only certain dents
- chooses only certain panels
- substantially reduces the repair
- changes the repair method
- removes documented operations
- materially changes the estimated job

then the estimating fee **may be credited depending on the final repair scope**.

This prevents a comprehensive professional estimating service from automatically becoming a discount against a much smaller or unrelated job.

Suggested customer-facing wording:

**When Peak Country performs the repair substantially as estimated, your applicable estimating fee is credited toward the repair. If the repair scope is changed or reduced, any estimate credit will depend on the work ultimately authorized.**

# 20. STANDARD MOBILE SERVICE AREA

The standard included mobile-service zone is:

## **25-mile geographic radius from a fixed reference point in downtown Greeley, Colorado**

This is now the established standard.

The radius must be measured from one permanent reference point.

It is **not**:

- 25 miles from Greeley's city limits
- 25 driving miles
- a rough approximation
- based on the closest point in Greeley

The fixed reference point should be a neutral public downtown location.

It should never reveal or imply the owner's residential address.

---

# 21. SERVICE AREA PAGE

The website should contain a prominent:

## Service Area

page/tab.

Its centerpiece should be an interactive map displaying the **25-mile standard mobile-service radius**.

The map should:

- clearly display the circle
- show recognizable cities
- show major roads
- allow zooming and panning
- function properly on mobile
- use the permanent downtown Greeley reference point
- avoid implying a residential business location

The map is intended to remove ambiguity.

A customer should be able to visually determine whether they are inside the standard included mobile area.

---

# 22. MEANING OF THE 25-MILE CIRCLE

The circle does **not** define where Peak Country will or will not work.

It defines where normal mobile travel is included.

Core message:

## Inside the circle

Standard mobile travel is included where applicable.

## Outside the circle

Peak Country may still provide mobile service.

Depending on:

- distance
- repair scope
- service required
- scheduling circumstances

a **small mobile service fee may apply**.

Any additional fee must be discussed with the customer before the appointment is confirmed.

The site must never automatically reject a customer simply because they are outside the circle.

---

# 23. SERVICE AREA CUSTOMER LANGUAGE

Recommended concept:

## Standard Mobile Service Area

Peak Country provides mobile service throughout Northern Colorado. The area shown on the map represents our standard mobile-service zone where travel is included.

**Outside the circle? That's okay.**

Depending on your location and the repair or service needed, a small mobile service fee may apply. We'll discuss that with you before scheduling so there are no surprises.

Avoid customer-facing language such as:

- travel surcharge
- out-of-area penalty
- we don't service your area
- service unavailable

---

# 24. ADDRESS CHECKER

The future Service Area experience should preferably include:

## Check My Address

The system compares the customer address against the exact 25-mile geographic radius.

### Inside radius

Suggested response:

**You're within our standard mobile-service area. Mobile travel is included where applicable.**

### Outside radius

Suggested response:

**You're outside our standard mobile-service area, but that's okay—we may still be able to come to you. Depending on distance and the service needed, a small mobile-service fee may apply.**

The customer should then continue normally.

Never automatically terminate the lead.

---

# 25. MOBILE SERVICE + ESTIMATING FEES

Published paid estimating/documentation fees include:

- professional technician time
- applicable inspection
- applicable written documentation
- applicable photographs/documentation
- normal mobile service within the standard 25-mile zone

Recommended concept:

**Listed estimating fees include the professional inspection, applicable documentation, and mobile service within our standard service area. If you're outside the standard area, a small mobile-service fee may apply depending on distance and the service required. Any additional fee will be confirmed before the appointment.**

Avoid saying simply:

**All charges included**

because unusual circumstances may legitimately require discussion.

---

# 26. GEOGRAPHIC MARKETING VS. MOBILE FEE ZONE

The 25-mile circle is a pricing/service-delivery boundary, not necessarily the complete marketing territory.

Peak Country can still create relevant local content and serve customers throughout Northern Colorado.

Major communities may still be referenced appropriately for SEO and customer clarity.

However:

**City names do not define whether mobile travel is included.**

The map does.

This prevents ambiguity involving large cities whose centers may fall within 25 miles while some neighborhoods lie beyond the circle.
