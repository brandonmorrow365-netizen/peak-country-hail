## 8. Estimating payment and credit rules

No money is collected merely because a customer clicks Get Started, submits a form, sends photos, asks about insurance, requests an assessment/inspection, or asks questions.

Paid estimating/documentation occurs only after the service is identified, the service and price are explained, and the customer agrees.

Do not use the older public phrase **“substantially as estimated.”**

## 10. Insurance role

Peak Country's role is centered on the vehicle and the repair.

Peak Country may evaluate damage, prepare repair estimates, review existing estimates, research vehicle-specific requirements, prepare repair-related documentation/supplements, provide photographs and OEM/technical support where appropriate, and communicate repair-related information with the customer, insurer, or repair facility when appropriate.

Peak Country does not decide insurance coverage and should not present itself as the customer's insurance company, insurance agent, or public adjuster.

General disclaimer: **Insurance coverage, reimbursement, deductibles, limitations, exclusions, and other benefits are subject to the terms and conditions of the customer's individual insurance policy.**

Preparing an estimate, supplement, repair plan, or technical support does not guarantee that a particular amount or operation will be covered or reimbursed.

## 12. Limited Lifetime Workmanship Warranty

Qualifying PDR repairs are backed by a **Limited Lifetime Workmanship Warranty for as long as the original customer owns the vehicle.**

The warranty is non-transferable and limited to Peak Country workmanship on the specific repair performed, including relevant R&I workmanship where included.

Customers should report suspected workmanship concerns within **30 calendar days after discovery or when they reasonably should have discovered the issue** and allow Peak Country the opportunity to inspect before another party alters the affected area.

The warranty does not cover new/unrelated damage, subsequent hail/collision, pre-existing conditions, previous repairs, factory/component failures, later alteration, or incidental/consequential expenses to the extent permitted by law.

PDR access may use factory openings or appropriately created access points.

Where repair access affects existing corrosion protection, use appropriate professional products and recognized repair practices to protect accessible affected areas. Do not claim perfect future corrosion prevention or “OEM approved” products unless specifically documented.

## 14. Professional mobile-service standard

Mobile service is designed around convenience without compromising conditions needed for a quality repair.

Many repairs may be performed outdoors when conditions are appropriate, but indoor/covered conditions are preferred when practical.

Outdoor limits:
- no normal outdoor repair in rain or snow
- excessive wind may require relocation/rescheduling
- temperatures below roughly 50–60°F may require relocation/rescheduling depending on repair type

Peak Country brings necessary PDR lighting, normally does not require customer electrical power, can perform normal R&I mobile, requires reasonable access to the vehicle, may require keys/authorization to move it, and prefers a reasonably clean vehicle.

Hail repairs generally benefit from/require a controlled indoor environment.

## 15. Repair evaluation standard

Do not use rigid dent-size limits.

Evaluate case by case based on factors such as size/depth/shape, impact severity, metal stretch, body-line involvement, edges/hems/braces/reinforced areas, paint condition, vehicle age, paint thickness/MIL readings when useful, previous refinishing/bodywork/PDR, material, specialized materials such as aluminum/HSS/UHSS, access, required disassembly, OEM/manufacturer information, safety/structural considerations, and customer goals.

Previously repaired/refinished panels are higher risk because underlying prep/materials may be unknown.

Aluminum is not automatically excluded. Glue pulling is a normal PDR technique when appropriate. Controlled heat may be used primarily to support paint flexibility.

PDR must not override safety, structural integrity, or relevant OEM restrictions.

Peak Country may recommend conventional repair, replacement, or a hybrid repair plan when more appropriate. Peak Country does not currently perform conventional body repair or panel replacement in house.

Full repair and improvement repair are distinct. Improvement repairs must clearly set expectations and limitations.

Core principle: **PDR is recommended when it is technically appropriate for the damage, vehicle construction, paint condition, access, and customer goals—not simply because the dent can physically be pushed or pulled.**
