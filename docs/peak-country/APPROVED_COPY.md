## Insurance FAQ

### Can Peak Country work with an insurance-related repair?

Yes.

Peak Country can evaluate the damage, prepare repair estimates, document repair requirements, research applicable OEM procedures, prepare supplements, and provide supporting repair information for insurance-related hail, PDR, and collision repairs.

Documentation can be provided to you, your insurance company, or another repair facility when appropriate.

### What if I already have an insurance estimate?

Bring it with you.

An existing estimate can be a useful starting point, but it may not include every operation ultimately required to complete the repair.

Peak Country can compare the estimate with the actual vehicle and review items such as additional or previously undocumented damage, missing repair operations, R&I requirements, repair-method changes, OEM procedures and position statements, vehicle-specific technical information, and supplemental repair requirements.

If additional documentation is needed, Peak Country can prepare a professional estimate review or supplement.

### Why might Peak Country’s estimate be different from my insurance estimate?

Estimate differences can occur for several legitimate reasons.

The vehicle may have received a more detailed inspection, additional damage may have been discovered, disassembly may be required, or vehicle-specific repair information may identify additional operations or a different repair method.

Peak Country documents **what is being recommended and why** so the repair plan can be evaluated based on the vehicle and repair requirements rather than only the final dollar amount.

### What is a supplement?

A supplement documents repair requirements that were not included in an earlier estimate or that became known after additional inspection, research, or disassembly.

Peak Country can prepare supporting documentation when these differences are identified.

### Will Peak Country communicate with my insurance company?

When appropriate, yes.

Peak Country can provide repair estimates, photographs, supplements, OEM information, and other repair-related documentation and can communicate about the vehicle damage and proposed repair.

Our role is to accurately **evaluate, research, and document the repair**.

No.

## Repair Evaluation

### Is Paintless Dent Repair Right for My Vehicle?

Not every dent should automatically be repaired with PDR.

Peak Country evaluates the damage, vehicle construction, paint condition, panel material, access, repair requirements, and your goals before recommending a repair.

The question is not simply: **“Can this dent be pushed out?”**

The better question is: **“Is PDR the right repair for this vehicle and this situation?”**

An experienced PDR professional may consider dent size/depth/shape, impact severity, metal movement/stretch, body-line involvement, panel edges/hems/braces, specialized materials, paint condition, vehicle age, previous bodywork/refinishing, tool access, required disassembly, manufacturer repair information, expected level of repair, and customer goals.

There is no single dent-size rule that determines whether PDR will work.

PDR is designed to preserve the existing finish, so paint condition is an important part of evaluation. Paint-thickness measurements may be used when helpful. Previously repaired or refinished panels are generally higher risk because underlying preparation/materials cannot always be seen beforehand.

Body-line dents, sharper impacts, larger dents, and damage near panel edges can often be repaired or significantly improved, depending on the combination of damage severity, stretch, paint condition, age, material, access, construction, and customer expectations.

Modern vehicles may use aluminum, high-strength steel, ultra-high-strength steel, and other specialized materials. Material type is part of the decision, not an automatic reason to approve or reject PDR.

Limited backside access does not automatically prevent PDR. Depending on the repair, Peak Country may use traditional tools, glue pulling, component R&I, factory access points, appropriate repair-access methods, or a combination.

Controlled heat may be used primarily to help maintain paint flexibility.

Previous bodywork does not automatically rule out PDR, but it can increase uncertainty and risk.

Hail damage is evaluated panel by panel and as a complete vehicle. Not every panel necessarily requires the same repair method.

If PDR is not the best choice, Peak Country will say so. Some damage is better suited for conventional repair, panel replacement, a blended plan, or another qualified repair facility. Peak Country does not currently perform conventional body repair or panel replacement in house.

When appropriate, Peak Country can discuss an **improvement repair** rather than a full repair, with expected result and limitations established before authorization.

Cosmetic repair should never override vehicle safety. Relevant manufacturer procedures, structural areas, reinforced components, safety-system locations, sensor locations, repair restrictions, and material-specific information may affect the recommendation.

**Evaluate the damage. Understand the vehicle. Consider your goals. Choose the repair method that makes sense.**

Peak Country recommends PDR when it is appropriate for the vehicle—not simply because the dent can physically be pushed or pulled.
